# Anatomy of a widget

Every field draws itself in one of two modes. In **view mode** it's a single line carrying the answer, with any supporting lines beneath it. Open it and it switches to **edit mode**, where the widget takes over the space to the right of the label and draws whatever it needs to collect the answer.

This page names each part and gives it a number, so a bug report can say "the constraint line is wrong" and mean exactly one thing. The numbers are fixed across every diagram below, so the **constraint** (13) carries the same number wherever it appears.

The names describe what you see, never how it's built. A file browser's list is captioned with the directory you're in, but the part is a **caption** (15) rather than a "directory line", because the next browser to caption its list won't be showing you a directory. Each one is drawn by a theme method - an [atom](/themes) - named after the part it draws.

:::note

The frames are real captured output. The vocabulary is the settled target and the source hasn't caught up with all of it yet, so [what the code calls these today](#what-the-code-calls-these-today) records the gap, and [open questions](#open-questions) tracks what's still undecided.

:::

## View mode

A panel stacks its fields as rows, each one in view mode until you open it. Five fields of a delivery form are visible here, with more continuing past the bottom edge.

<p align="center">
  <ThemedImage sources={{light: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-row-light-static.svg'), dark: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-row-dark-static.svg')}} alt="Five rows of a form with their parts named" width="100%" />
</p>

- **1** breadcrumb - the trail of panel titles. It's the titles of every panel you've descended through plus the one you're in, so it gains a segment when you open a sub-panel and drops one when you come back.
- **20** separator - the glyph between breadcrumb segments. It's drawn by the theme, so it isn't always a chevron.
- **2** field selector - which field has focus. Exactly one row has it at a time, it moves with <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd>, and the row it's on is drawn brighter - the glyph and that emphasis are one signal in two forms.
- **3** label - the field's name. The one part of a row that never changes while you work.
- **4** value - the settled answer. It shows what was accepted, so it stays empty until the field is answered and never shows what you're mid-way through typing - that's the **draft** (17).
- **5** description - the field's explanatory text, under its row. Declared once on the field and shown whether or not the row has focus, so it never changes while you work.
- **7** overflow - content continues above or below. It appears only when the rows don't fit the panel, and points the way there's more.
- **8** legend - the keys available right now. It rewrites itself as focus moves, so a field in edit mode lists different keys from the panel around it.

**One number, several arrows.** The **label** (3) and the **value** (4) each carry an arrow per row, because every row has one of each - they belong to a row, not to a particular field. A part that occurs several times keeps a single number; numbering each occurrence would read as several different parts.

The **field selector** (2) is the exception. It gets one arrow, because only one field holds focus at a time. That row is drawn differently too: `Basket contents` has a brighter **label** (3) and **value** (4) than the rows below it. The glyph and that emphasis are the same signal in two forms, and both come from the theme.

## Help

A field can carry longer text than a **description** (5) has room for. That text is its **help** (6), and it isn't on screen until you ask for it: press <kbd>?</kbd> on a field and it opens in a popup.

- **6** help - the field's long-form text, shown on request. Unlike the **description** (5), it can run to several paragraphs, because a popup has the room a row doesn't.

Keeping it behind a keystroke is what lets it be thorough. A **description** (5) has to earn its line in a stack of rows, so it stays a sentence; **help** (6) is asked for, so it can take as long as the field deserves.

## Edit mode

Edit mode hands the region right of the label to the widget. A multiple-choice field with bounds and option detail draws the fullest set of parts.

<p align="center">
  <ThemedImage sources={{light: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-editor-light-static.svg'), dark: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-editor-dark-static.svg')}} alt="A multiple-choice field in edit mode with its parts named" width="100%" />
</p>

- **9** entry marker - the per-entry chosen-state glyph. It shows what's chosen, not what's highlighted, so it only changes when you actually pick something - <kbd>Space</kbd> in a multiple-choice list.
- **10** entry - one line of the list the widget offers. What fills the list varies by widget: a fixed set, a set filtered as you type, or one fetched by a query.
- **11** entry selector - which **entry** (10) has focus. It moves with <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd>, and moving it chooses nothing on its own.
- **12** entry note - a qualifier on an **entry** (10), such as why it's unavailable. It appears only on entries that carry a reason.
- **13** constraint - what the field expects, before anything is rejected. It's there from the moment edit mode opens, and the **error** (19) replaces it the instant a value is refused.
- **14** entry detail - the focused **entry** (10)'s own explanatory text. It's rewritten every time the **entry selector** (11) moves, and is simply absent for entries that declare none.

**Selecting and marking are different things.** A selector shows where you are; a marker shows what you've marked. That's why the **field selector** (2) and the **entry selector** (11) are the glyphs that follow your movement, while the **entry marker** (9) is the box that records a decision. Moving a selector chooses nothing.

The pairs are deliberate. The **field selector** (2) shows which field you're on and the **entry selector** (11) which entry within it; the **description** (5) is the field's own text and the **entry detail** (14) an entry's. Same idea at two levels, so a parent's name is never reused for its child.

Order matters here too. The **entry detail** (14) sits directly under the list and above the **constraint** (13), because it changes as focus moves. A line that follows the cursor belongs against the list it follows; a limit that never moves can wait below it.

## Edit mode: typing rather than choosing

Some widgets collect an answer by typing instead of by choosing from a list, so they have no **entry** (10) at all. The [Template](/widgets/template) widget shown here fills the named slots of a fixed pattern, and it introduces the three parts that appear whenever you type.

<p align="center">
  <ThemedImage sources={{light: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-text-light-static.svg'), dark: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-text-dark-static.svg')}} alt="A Template field in edit mode with its parts named" width="100%" />
</p>

- **16** caret - the insertion point within the **draft** (17). It shows where the next keystroke lands, and in the [Template](/widgets/template) widget it's what moves between slots as you fill them.
- **17** draft - the text you're typing, before it's accepted. It becomes the **value** (4) when you accept, and is discarded if you cancel.
- **18** state - what the widget is doing right now. It tracks the widget rather than the field, so it changes as you work - here it names the slot you're filling.

The **draft** (17) and the **value** (4) are the same answer at two moments: what you're typing, and what was accepted.

The **state** (18) is easy to confuse with the **description** (5) below it, so it's worth being precise. The first tracks the widget and changes while you work - here it names the slot you're filling - while the second belongs to the field and never moves.

## Edit mode: browsing a list

The [FilePicker](/widgets/filepicker) widget draws the same skeleton over a directory listing, and adds a **caption** (15) above it.

<p align="center">
  <ThemedImage sources={{light: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-filepicker-light-static.svg'), dark: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-filepicker-dark-static.svg')}} alt="An open file picker with its parts named" width="100%" />
</p>

- **15** caption - what the list below is showing. It's rewritten whenever the list changes underneath it, so here it names the directory you've navigated into.

The lines in the list are files and directories rather than options, and they're still **entries** (10): the name describes the line the widget offers, not where its content came from. The **caption** (15) names a directory in this widget because that's what a file browser browses, but the part - and the atom that draws it - stays generic.

## Constraint and error: one line, two states

The **constraint** (13) and the **error** (19) share a single physical line. Below is one [FilePicker](/widgets/filepicker) field, limited to files of at most 64 bytes, photographed twice.

Nothing has been picked yet, so the line states what the field expects:

<p align="center">
  <ThemedImage sources={{light: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-constraint-light-static.svg'), dark: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-constraint-dark-static.svg')}} alt="A file picker showing its constraint before anything is rejected" width="100%" />
</p>

Now `harvest.csv` is picked, and at 88 bytes it breaks that limit. The same line, in the same place, turns into the **error** (19). The **constraint** (13) doesn't move down or stay above it - it's replaced:

<p align="center">
  <ThemedImage sources={{light: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-error-light-static.svg'), dark: useBaseUrl('/anatomy-error-dark-static.svg')}} alt="The same file picker showing an error in place of its constraint" width="100%" />
</p>

- **19** error - why the value was rejected. It appears only after a refused accept, and clears the moment the value becomes acceptable, handing the line back to the **constraint** (13).

Read the two lines side by side and the distinction is the whole point:

| | Line | Says |
|---|---|---|
| **constraint** (13) | `Files only. Max 64 B.` | what the field will accept, before you act |
| **error** (19) | `Choose a file no larger than 64 B.` | why what you just did was refused |

A **constraint** (13) describes the field; an **error** (19) describes your value. That's also why they're built differently. The first is an unframed phrase - `a file no larger than 64 B` - that its caller wraps, so the same phrase becomes `Choose ...` in the picker, `Select ...` in a bounded list and `must be ...` headlessly. The second is already a whole message, and is shown as it stands.

## Where the names disagree

**An atom is named after the part it draws.** `Field::$label` and `ThemeInterface::label()` line up because a theme's vocabulary should be the vocabulary a reader uses for what's on screen. Where the code and this page disagree on a name, the code is what moves.

**`hints()` returns the legend, not a hint.** `WidgetInterface::hints()` has nothing to do with `Field::$hint` or with `ThemeInterface::hint()` - it returns the keys of the **legend** (8). It becomes `WidgetInterface::legend()`.

**`hint` will name one thing.** `Field::$hint` becomes `Field::$help`, since its text is the **help** (6) and belongs in the popup rather than on a line under the row. That leaves `ThemeInterface::hint()` drawing exactly one part, the **constraint** (13), so the atom takes that name too.

**`description` names two parts.** The **description** (5) and the **entry detail** (14) both render through `ThemeInterface::description()`, so restyling one restyles the other.

**The constraint has two names.** `SelectionBoundedTrait::selectionHint()` and `FilePickerWidget::constraintHint()` both draw the **constraint** (13), with the same body and two different names.

**`marker` covers two levels.** The **field selector** (2) and the **entry selector** (11) are both `ThemeInterface::marker()`, so a theme can't restyle one without the other - and neither is a marker any more.

**`error` names four things.** `ThemeInterface::error()` is a style atom, `WidgetInterface::error()` is widget state, `Field::templateError()` and friends produce a message, and `Engine::optionsError()` builds an exception.

**`footer` is drawn outside the footer.** The **state** (18) uses `ThemeInterface::footer()`, which otherwise styles the **legend** (8) at the bottom of the frame.

**Two parts have no name at all.** The **entry** (10) and the **entry marker** (9) are drawn inline, with no atom behind them.

## Open questions

Working notes, kept here only until each one is settled. The vocabulary above is decided; these are the edges still being argued.

**Should the entry detail be tied to the focused entry by color?** The **entry detail** (14) is the one part rewritten every time the **entry selector** (11) moves, and today it borrows `description()` - the same atom as the **description** (5), which never moves. Giving it its own atom in the selector's accent would say "this belongs to the row you're pointing at". The catch: green is already the **value** (4), and the selector's cyan is also the border, so the accent isn't free.

**Should the field selector and the entry selector be separate atoms?** They're one method today, so a theme can't restyle the field cursor without also restyling the one inside every list. They're deliberately named as a parent/child pair, which argues they should be stylable as one.

**Should the entry and the entry marker get atoms of their own?** The **entry** (10) and the **entry marker** (9) are drawn inline today, so neither can be restyled without overriding the whole list renderer.

**Should the state stop borrowing `footer()`?** The **state** (18) is drawn with the atom that styles the **legend** (8) at the bottom of the frame, which is a different part in a different place.

**What replaces the keyboard overlay?** <kbd>?</kbd> currently opens a list of every widget type's keys, which is being dropped in favour of the **help** (6) popup - the **legend** (8) already shows the keys that apply right now, and rewrites itself as focus moves. Worth confirming nothing is lost with it.
